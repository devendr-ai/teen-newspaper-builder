Oil Near $110 as Houthis Claim New Strikes on Saudi Arabia

Crude flows through the Gulf region have fallen sharply.

The Houthis, an armed group from Yemen, said on Monday that they had attacked Saudi energy, airport and military sites. They named an Aramco facility in Yanbu, the Shaybah oilfield and King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the claims, and Reuters could not verify them.

Oil markets reacted. Brent crude, the benchmark price for internationally traded oil, settled at $109.55 a barrel on Monday and traded near $110 on Tuesday. Kpler, a data firm, says crude flows through the Gulf region averaged about 8.5 million barrels a day last week, down from more than 20 million the week before. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said an oil tanker exploded after hitting a naval mine in the southern Strait of Hormuz on Sunday.

Governments are responding. Egypt says it stands ready to support Saudi Arabia against the Houthis, but its parliament delayed until October 26 a vote on letting its armed forces help if asked.

For revision, hold these numbers: $109.55 for Monday's Brent settle; about 8.5 million barrels a day last week against more than 20 million the week before; and October 26 for Egypt's delayed vote. Notice the wording: the Houthis said they carried out the attacks, and Saudi authorities had not confirmed them. In news, a claim is not the same as a confirmed fact.

Exam tip: a strait is a narrow stretch of water linking two larger seas. Oil tankers leaving the Persian Gulf must pass through the Strait of Hormuz, which is why trouble there shows up quickly in oil prices.

Why it matters

Oil is traded worldwide, so danger near big producers can raise fuel prices for households everywhere, including in India.
