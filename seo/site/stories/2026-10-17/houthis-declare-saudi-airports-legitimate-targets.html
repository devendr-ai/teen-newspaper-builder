Houthis Declare Saudi Airports “Legitimate Targets”

Saudi officials count at least 20 deaths at airports between October 6 and 11.

The Houthis, an Iran-aligned movement holding Sanaa and parts of north-west Yemen since 2014, are fighting Saudi-backed Yemeni government forces. A UN-brokered truce in place since 2022 has collapsed. After a strike on Sanaa airport, the Houthi leader set out a tit-for-tat policy: “airports for airports, ports for ports, and a blockade for a blockade.”

The Saudi-backed Yemeni government says the Sanaa strike was meant to stop an Iranian plane landing there. The Houthis have declared the kingdom’s airports and airspace “legitimate targets,” except Mecca and Medina, and say they have also hit Aramco facilities, including a gas plant and a refinery.

Saudi civil aviation authorities report at least 20 deaths and 356 injuries at airports between October 6 and 11. The deadliest strike, on Riyadh’s King Khalid International Airport on October 10, killed at least 12, including an American citizen. Dammam, Abha, Jazan and Najran airports were also hit. On Wednesday the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted a missile fired toward Riyadh. Houthi claims could not be independently verified.

Why it matters

The Bab el-Mandeb strait, at the Red Sea’s southern end, is a key route between the Mediterranean and Asia, and its importance has grown because the Iran conflict has effectively closed Hormuz. Control of it is disputed.
