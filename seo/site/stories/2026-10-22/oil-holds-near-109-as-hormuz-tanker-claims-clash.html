Oil Holds Near $109 as Hormuz Tanker Claims Clash

Houthi and Iranian claims keep supply fears alive; the US denies the mine reports.

Brent crude, the international benchmark price for oil, closed at $108.82 a barrel on Tuesday 20 October, according to CNBC. US West Texas Intermediate, the American benchmark, settled at $97.15. The latest rise followed claims by the Houthis, an Iran-aligned movement in Yemen, that they had attacked several Saudi Arabian facilities on Monday. Saudi authorities did not immediately confirm the claims, and Reuters could not verify them.

The second pressure point is the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow sea lane between Iran and Oman. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said their naval mines had stopped three tankers on routes Tehran considers unauthorised, and that two caught fire. US Central Command rejected that account, saying the claims that tankers hit mines were false.

Diplomacy continues alongside. Iran and the United States remain in indirect talks through Pakistan and Qatar. Iran's foreign minister said the strait will stay closed unless Washington accepts Tehran's conditions. Each side is therefore claiming the other must move first, which leaves traders pricing in risk rather than facts.

Why it matters

Oil is priced globally, so threats to supply in the Gulf can lift fuel costs, inflation and interest-rate decisions in countries far from the fighting, including India.
